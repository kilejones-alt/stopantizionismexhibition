01WHY THIS EXHIBITION

For centuries, the history of Jew-hatred has been understood through two principal eras: anti-Judaism and antisemitism. Yet one of the most consequential chapters in this history remains largely absent from museums, educational institutions, and public discourse—the history of modern antizionism.

At a time of unprecedented global concern over rising Jew-hatred, understanding the historical development of modern antizionism has become not only academically important, but an educational imperative.

The Third Era: Antizionism and the History of Jew-Hatred is being developed as a museum-quality educational exhibition intended to address this gap. Building on Dr. Naya Lekht's historical framework, which identifies antizionism as a third era in the evolution of Jew-hatred, the exhibition examines modern antizionism through archival research, primary-source documentation, multimedia presentations, and testimony.

02THE MAKING OF ANTIZIONISM

Three Streams, One River

At the heart of the exhibition is a historical narrative showing how three distinct ideological streams converged to shape modern antizionism.

01

The Arab World

Opposition to Jewish national self-determination became a central political and ideological movement.

02

Nazi Ideology

Propaganda and demonization of Jews continued to influence anti-Jewish narratives after the Second World War.

03

The Soviet Union

Traditional antisemitic narratives were recast in the language of anti-Zionism, anti-colonialism, and political activism, then exported internationally.

CONVERGENCEModern Antizionism

Visitors will trace how these streams converged into an international movement that continues to influence public discourse, academia, media, international institutions, and social movements today.

03INSIDE THE EXHIBITION
I

The Three Eras of Jew-Hatred

Introducing the historical progression from anti-Judaism to antisemitism and, ultimately, antizionism.

II

The Making of Antizionism

Documenting the historical origins, ideological development, and global dissemination of antizionism through the Arab world, Nazi Germany, and the Soviet Union.

III

Antizionism Today

Examining how historical narratives continue to shape contemporary society and debates surrounding democracy, human rights, education, and Jewish communities worldwide.

04NEW YORK CITY

A Public Engagement

Planned for New York City—home to one of the world's largest Jewish communities outside Israel and a major center of culture, education, media, and public dialogue—the exhibition is envisioned as a two-to-three-week public engagement welcoming students, educators, universities, policymakers, journalists, community leaders, and the general public.

Planned Programming

  • Opening symposium with internationally recognized scholars
  • Testimony from individuals who experienced antizionism as state policy
  • Documentary film screenings
  • Guided tours
  • Educational programming for schools and universities
05BUILT ON PRIMARY SOURCES

Historical Rigor, Public Understanding

Every major historical claim presented throughout the exhibition will be supported by primary-source documentation and informed by scholarly research, ensuring historical rigor while encouraging informed dialogue, critical thinking, and a deeper understanding of one of the defining ideological movements of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.

06BEYOND NEW YORK

A Foundation for a Broader Educational Initiative

While premiering in New York City, the exhibition is envisioned as the foundation for a broader educational initiative, including a traveling exhibition, digital educational resources, and future presentations at museums, universities, and cultural institutions across North America and beyond. By preserving and presenting this overlooked chapter of modern history, the project seeks to create a lasting educational legacy for future generations.
