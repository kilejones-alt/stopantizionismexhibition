Public Exhibition · Symposium · Film · Testimony · Education

01 · The Historical Gap

Why this exhibition

For centuries, the history of Jew-hatred has been understood through two principal eras: anti-Judaism and antisemitism. Yet one of the most consequential chapters in this history remains largely absent from museums, educational institutions, and public discourse: the history of modern antizionism.

At a time of unprecedented global concern over rising Jew-hatred, understanding the historical development of modern antizionism has become not only academically important, but an educational imperative.

The Third Era: Antizionism and the History of Jew-Hatred is a groundbreaking, museum-quality educational exhibition created to address this critical gap. Built on Dr. Naya Lekht’s pioneering historical framework, which identifies antizionism as a third era in the historical evolution of Jew-hatred, the exhibition will be the first comprehensive public presentation to examine the development of modern antizionism through archival research, primary-source documentation, multimedia presentations, and survivor testimony.

02 · The Central Narrative

Three streams, one river

At the heart of the exhibition is Three Streams, One River: The Making of Antizionism, a historical narrative demonstrating how three distinct ideological streams converged to shape the modern antizionist movement.

I

The Arab world

Opposition to Jewish national self-determination developed into a central political and ideological movement with regional and international influence.

II

Nazi ideology

Propaganda and the demonization of Jews continued to shape and influence anti-Jewish narratives after the Second World War.

III

The Soviet Union

Traditional antisemitic narratives were transformed into the language of anti-Zionism, anti-colonialism, and political activism, then exported throughout the world.

Visitors will encounter how these streams converged into a powerful international movement that continues to influence public discourse, academia, media, international institutions, and social movements today.

03 · Exhibition Architecture

Three interconnected sections

01

The Three Eras of Jew-Hatred

An introduction to the historical progression from anti-Judaism to antisemitism and, ultimately, to antizionism.

02

The Making of Antizionism

A documented account of the historical origins, ideological development, and global dissemination of antizionism through the Arab world, Nazi Germany, and the Soviet Union.

03

Antizionism Today

An examination of how these historical narratives continue to shape contemporary society and influence debates surrounding democracy, human rights, education, and Jewish communities worldwide.

04 · The New York Presentation

A public engagement

Hosted in New York City—home to one of the world’s largest Jewish communities outside Israel and one of the world’s leading centers of culture, education, media, and public dialogue—the exhibition is envisioned as a two-to-three-week public engagement welcoming students, educators, universities, policymakers, journalists, community leaders, and the general public.

Programming will include an opening symposium featuring internationally recognized scholars, survivor testimony from individuals who experienced antizionism as state policy, documentary film screenings, guided tours, and educational programming for schools and universities.

05 · Historical Method

Evidence before assertion

Every major historical claim presented throughout the exhibition will be supported by primary-source documentation and informed by scholarly research. The exhibition is designed to maintain historical rigor while encouraging informed dialogue, critical thinking, and a deeper understanding of one of the defining ideological movements of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.

06 · A Lasting Initiative

Beyond the premiere

While premiering in New York City, the exhibition is envisioned as the foundation for a broader educational initiative, including a traveling exhibition, digital educational resources, and future presentations at museums, universities, and cultural institutions across North America and beyond.

By preserving and presenting this overlooked chapter of modern history, the project seeks to create a lasting educational legacy for future generations.
