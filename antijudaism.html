A Genealogy of Christian Antijudaism

Antijudaism preceded modern antisemitism by centuries.

Its central language was theological. Jews were represented as spiritually blind, collectively guilty, stubborn in their rejection of Christianity, and dangerous to Christian society. Over time, doctrine became image, accusation, law, folklore, and violence.

The underlying structure endured: Jews were imagined not simply as people who practiced another religion, but as an alien presence capable of corrupting, contaminating, or attacking the sacred order around them.

c. 1230 — Supersession

Synagoga, Strasbourg Cathedral

Creator: Unknown Gothic sculptor; photograph by Rama
Date: c. 1230 (sculpture); 2006 photograph

Creator

The sculptural program at Strasbourg Cathedral used personification to stage the Christian claim that the Church had superseded Judaism.

Object

The blindfolded figure of Synagoga personifies Judaism as defeated and spiritually unseeing, paired at Strasbourg Cathedral with the triumphant Ecclesia.

Archive

Strasbourg Cathedral; digital photograph by Rama, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0 France.

1305–1340 — Marked Difference

Süßkind of Trimberg, Codex Manesse

Creator: Master of the Codex Manesse, Additional Painter I
Date: between 1305 and 1340

Creator

The Codex Manesse is a richly illuminated anthology of Middle High German lyric poetry assembled in Heidelberg; Süßkind is its only identified Jewish poet.

Object

The manuscript portrait identifies the Jewish poet Süßkind of Trimberg through dress, beard, and the pointed Jewish hat while placing him before a secular authority.

Archive

Codex Manesse, fol. 355r; Heidelberg University Library, Cod. Pal. germ. 848; Wikimedia Commons reproduction.

1349 — Pestilence and Collective Guilt

Burning of Jews during the Black Death

Creator: Medieval manuscript illuminator
Date: 1349

Creator

The image belongs to the visual record of the persecutions that accompanied the plague crisis across parts of Europe.

Object

An illuminated manuscript scene represents Jews being burned during the Black Death persecutions, when Jewish communities were accused of poisoning wells and spreading plague.

Archive

Medieval manuscript image; public-domain reproduction circulated through Wikimedia Commons.

1467–1468 — Host Desecration

Miracle of the Profaned Host

Creator: Paolo Uccello
Date: 1467–1468

Creator

Paolo Uccello, a major Florentine painter, produced the six-part predella for the Corpus Domini confraternity in Urbino.

Object

One panel from Uccello’s predella narrates the host-desecration legend, a story in which Jews were accused of attacking the consecrated Eucharist.

Archive

Paolo Uccello, Miracle of the Profaned Host, tempera on panel; public-domain reproduction via Wikimedia Commons / Web Gallery of Art.

1493 — Ritual Murder

Simon of Trent in the Nuremberg Chronicle

Creator: Workshop of Michael Wolgemut and Wilhelm Pleydenwurff
Date: 1493

Creator

The Nuremberg Chronicle, compiled by Hartmann Schedel and illustrated by the Wolgemut-Pleydenwurff workshop, was one of the most ambitious illustrated books of the incunable period.

Object

The woodcut illustrates the false ritual-murder accusation surrounding Simon of Trent, depicting Jews as participants in the killing of a Christian child.

Archive

Hartmann Schedel, Nuremberg Chronicle, 1493; public-domain reproduction via Wikimedia Commons.

Closing

Christian antijudaism created a vocabulary that survived long after medieval theology ceased to organize European public life.

Blindness. Contamination. Ritual cruelty. Collective guilt. Secret violence. Hostility to the surrounding society. Modern antisemitism would secularize many of these ideas.

The enemy of God would become the enemy of the nation, the race, and civilization.
